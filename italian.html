Italian is close to phonetic. Learn five vowels and three consonant rules and you can read almost any word aloud, even one you have never seen.

Italian has 21 letters: j, k, w, x and y appear only in loanwords. Every letter is pronounced, final vowels included, and there are almost no silent letters. The one exception is h, which is silent in native words but keeps a c or g hard: chi is “kee”, not “chee”.

Vowels are open and short: a as in father, e as in bed (or the “ay” of may, depending on the word), i as in see, o as in more (or the “aw” of law), u as in food. Do not glide them into diphthongs; no is a clean “noh”, not “now”.

    Eight phrases worth more than their words

    These are the sentences the hundred words are for. Most are built from the list above; a few bring their own verb or noun, which is the point at which a phrasebook takes over.

    • Quanto costa?

      How much is it?

      KWAN-toh KOS-tah

    • Dov’è il bagno?

      Where is the bathroom?

      doh-VEH eel BAH-nyoh

    • Non capisco.

      I don’t understand.

      non kah-PEES-koh

    • Parla inglese?

      Do you speak English?

      PAR-lah een-GLEH-zeh

    • Il conto, per favore.

      The bill, please.

      eel KON-toh pehr fah-VOH-reh

    • Un caffè, per favore.

      A coffee, please.

      oon kaf-FEH pehr fah-VOH-reh

    • Posso pagare con la carta?

      Can I pay by card?

      POS-soh pah-GAH-reh kon lah KAR-tah

    • Aiuto!

      Help!

      ah-YOO-toh

    One warning about the respelling. Italian vowels are shorter and cleaner than their English lookalikes. “CHOW” for ciao is a start, but the o is a pure “oh”, not the diphthong in English “cow”. Listen once, then imitate the vowel, not the spelling.

    Notes on this list

    • The hundred entries are a beginner’s working vocabulary, not a corpus frequency count. They were chosen for a short visit to Rome and grouped by use.
    • IPA transcriptions are broad and follow standard Italian; Roman speech varies, especially in the openness of e and o.
    • Stress, double consonants and the c/g rules follow standard Italian orthography; see the Oxford Italian Dictionary or Treccani for fuller treatment.
    • Audio uses the browser’s speech synthesis. If no Italian voice is installed, the system voice may read Italian with an English accent; it is a prompt, not a model.
